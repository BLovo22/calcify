How the Simplified Benefit Adjustments Work

Before full retirement age, the retirement benefit is reduced by 5/9 of 1% for each of the first 36 months and 5/12 of 1% for each additional month. After full retirement age, this calculator uses an 8% annual delayed retirement credit, prorated monthly, for people born in 1943 or later. Delayed credits stop at age 70.

Break-even is not a recommendation

A break-even age answers one narrow question: when does one stream of cumulative checks overtake another? It does not value insurance against living a very long time, survivor benefits, immediate cash needs, work rules, taxes, Medicare premiums, investment returns, or peace of mind.

Important Exclusions

  • No federal or state income tax, earnings test, Medicare premium, or benefit taxation calculation.
  • No spouse, divorced-spouse, survivor, disability, family-maximum, windfall, or government-pension analysis.
  • No investment return on earlier payments and no discounting of future payments.
  • The COLA field applies one uniform annual assumption from age 62 to all three claiming streams. The displayed monthly cards remain in base-year dollars; actual cost-of-living adjustments change and are not guaranteed.
  • The model assumes all delayed credits earned through the selected claiming age are reflected immediately. For some claims before age 70, SSA may not apply part of the current-year delayed credit until the following January.
  • Your Social Security Statement estimate and birth date should be used for a real decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is claiming at 70 always best?
No. Delaying increases the monthly amount, but a person gives up checks while waiting. Health, household income, survivor planning, work, and liquidity can change the decision.
Why is the age-62 reduction about 30% when FRA is 67?
Claiming 60 months early includes 36 months reduced at 5/9 of 1% and 24 additional months reduced at 5/12 of 1%, for a total reduction of about 30%.
Where do I find my full-retirement-age estimate?
Use your official Social Security Statement or my Social Security account. The estimate can change with future covered earnings.
