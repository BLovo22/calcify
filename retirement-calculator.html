How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter Your Current Age — the earlier you start, the more compound interest works for you.
  2. Choose Retirement Age — 65-67 is traditional. Earlier retirement requires a bigger nest egg.
  3. Enter Current Savings — your total across 401k, IRA, brokerage, etc.
  4. Enter Monthly Contribution — include your contribution plus any employer match.
  5. Set Expected Return and Inflation — the calculator grows both your portfolio and your income target in future dollars.
  6. Enter Desired Income — how much annual income you want in retirement, expressed in today's dollars.

Example: The 30-Year-Old Planning Ahead

You are 30 years old, have $50,000 saved, contribute $1,000/month at 7% returns, expect 3% inflation, and want the equivalent of $60,000/year in retirement.

Common Mistakes

❌ Not accounting for inflation. If you need $60,000/year today, at 3% inflation you will need about $169,000/year in 35 years. The calculator adjusts the 4% target accordingly.

❌ Overestimating returns. A 10-12% annual return may be too optimistic for a diversified retirement portfolio. Test a range of conservative assumptions.

❌ Forgetting employer match. If your employer adds $300 to your $1,000 monthly contribution, enter the full $1,300. Always include the match in your monthly amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do I need to retire?▼
The 4% rule is a starting point: multiply desired annual retirement income by 25. A $60,000 income requires $1,500,000 in today's dollars; this calculator then adjusts that target for inflation through your retirement date.
Am I saving enough?▼
A common guideline is saving 15-20% of gross income including employer match. If you earn $80,000/year, that means $12,000-$16,000 per year. Test several return and inflation assumptions rather than relying on one projection.
