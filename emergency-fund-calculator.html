Transparent planning rules

This is a planning heuristic, not an official requirement. The model starts with a lower range for steady income, a larger range for variable income, and the largest range for seasonal income. It then considers dependents and your expected time to replace income. The insurance deductible is added as a separate cash exposure instead of being disguised as another month of expenses.

Starter layer

Before pursuing the full target, a smaller cash buffer can reduce the chance that an ordinary surprise goes on a high-cost credit card.

Income layer

Monthly essential expenses multiplied by risk-adjusted months supports a job loss, client gap, medical leave, or seasonal slowdown.

Deductible layer

A known deductible or similar household exposure can occur while income is interrupted, so the calculator adds it to the expense reserve.

Context: the Federal Reserve reported that in 2025, 55% of adults said they had set aside enough emergency savings to cover three months of expenses. See the 2025 Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I count all monthly spending?
Use essential crisis spending, not your normal lifestyle total. Include housing, basic food, utilities, insurance, necessary transportation and healthcare, and minimum debt payments. Exclude expenses you could promptly pause.
Why might freelancers need more?
Income can decline without formal job loss, multiple clients may be connected to the same economy, invoices can be delayed, and paid leave or severance may be unavailable.
Should I pay debt before building the full fund?
Many people first build a starter buffer, then balance high-cost debt reduction with progress toward a fuller reserve. The right order depends on debt cost, income risk, insurance, and access to other safe liquidity.
