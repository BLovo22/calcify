2026 Car Loan Interest Deduction: Who Qualifies and How Much Can You Save?

Updated July 2026 · 10 min read

📑 Table of Contents

    For tax years 2025 through 2028, eligible taxpayers may deduct up to $10,000 per year of qualified passenger vehicle loan interest. The deduction is available to eligible itemizers and non-itemizers, but it is not available for every car or every loan.

    Qualification depends on when and why the loan originated, the vehicle's original use and final assembly, more-than-50% personal use, lien status, income, related-party restrictions, and allocation rules for mixed financing, mixed use, or refinancing. Use this guide as a planning overview and confirm current IRS instructions for your return.

    The deduction in one sentence

    An eligible taxpayer can deduct qualified interest paid or accrued on a qualifying passenger vehicle loan, subject to a $10,000 annual maximum and a modified adjusted gross income phaseout. The provision applies for tax years 2025 through 2028 under current law.

    This is an income deduction, not a tax credit. It reduces the income used to calculate tax; it does not normally reduce tax dollar for dollar.

    Basic loan requirements

    IRS Topic No. 505 states that the indebtedness must be incurred after December 31, 2024, for the purchase of the vehicle and secured by a first lien on it. Lease payments do not qualify. Interest must be qualified interest under the applicable rules, so the number on a general payment schedule is not enough by itself.

    Keep lender statements and purchase documents that establish loan origination, interest paid, security interest, and vehicle information. Follow IRS reporting instructions for the relevant tax year.

    Basic vehicle requirements

    The vehicle must be new, meaning its original use begins with the taxpayer. A used vehicle purchase does not qualify. Final assembly must occur in the United States. A dealership's location and the manufacturer's brand are not proof of final assembly, so verify the vehicle using the VIN and official resources referenced by the IRS.

    IRS materials describe qualified passenger vehicles as applicable cars, minivans, vans, sport utility vehicles, pickup trucks, or motorcycles with a gross vehicle weight rating below 14,000 pounds. The vehicle must be purchased for personal use. Business-use and mixed-use situations require careful review of the rules.

    The $10,000 annual cap

    Start with qualified interest actually paid or accrued for the year. The maximum amount before the income phaseout is $10,000. If you paid $2,400 of qualified interest, the starting amount is $2,400. If you paid $12,500, the starting amount is capped at $10,000.

    The cap applies to the deduction, not the vehicle price or loan balance. A large loan does not automatically produce a $10,000 deduction; only qualified interest for the year enters the calculation.

    How the MAGI phaseout works

    The phaseout begins when modified adjusted gross income exceeds $100,000 for filers other than married filing jointly, or $200,000 for married couples filing jointly. The otherwise allowable deduction is reduced by $200 for every $1,000, or portion of $1,000, above the threshold.

    For example, a single filer with $104,100 of MAGI is $4,100 above the threshold. Because a partial $1,000 counts, that is five phaseout increments, reducing the deduction by $1,000. If qualified interest before phaseout is $2,400, the estimated deduction becomes $1,400.

    A joint filer with $225,000 of MAGI is $25,000 above the threshold. The reduction is $5,000. If qualified interest before phaseout is $8,000, the estimated deduction is $3,000. The deduction cannot be reduced below zero.

    Estimating the tax benefit

    A rough estimate multiplies the allowable deduction by a marginal federal tax rate. If the deduction is $2,400 and the relevant marginal rate is 22%, the simple estimate is $528. If the deduction is $1,400 after phaseout, the same rough method gives $308.

    This multiplication is only a planning shortcut. Taxable income, other deductions, filing status, credits, limitations, and state tax treatment can change the actual result. Do not increase a vehicle budget by the maximum advertised deduction without first checking what you may actually qualify to deduct.

    A deduction should not justify an unaffordable vehicle

    Even a fully usable $10,000 deduction is not a $10,000 rebate. At a 22% marginal rate, the simple federal benefit would be about $2,200. Meanwhile, a more expensive vehicle can increase payment, insurance, sales tax, registration, depreciation, fuel, tires, and repair costs.

    Choose a vehicle based on total ownership cost first. Treat any tax benefit as secondary and uncertain until eligibility and tax impact are confirmed.

    Records to organize

    • Purchase contract showing the vehicle was acquired new.
    • Loan agreement and evidence the loan originated after December 31, 2024.
    • Documents showing the lender's first lien and annual qualified interest.
    • VIN and evidence used to verify final assembly in the United States.
    • Records supporting personal use and any required allocation.
    • Tax documents and worksheets used to calculate MAGI and the deduction.

    Common qualification mistakes

    • Assuming every new vehicle sold in the United States was assembled here.
    • Treating used-car interest or lease payments as qualified.
    • Using total loan payments instead of the qualified interest amount.
    • Ignoring the MAGI phaseout because interest is below $10,000.
    • Confusing a deduction with a dollar-for-dollar tax credit.
    • Estimating savings without checking the current IRS form and instructions.

    Bottom line

    The 2026 car loan interest deduction can reduce federal taxable income for an eligible new, U.S.-assembled, personal-use vehicle financed with a qualifying loan. The annual deduction is capped at $10,000 and can be reduced or eliminated as MAGI rises above $100,000, or $200,000 for joint filers.

    Use the Car Loan Interest Deduction Calculator for a planning estimate, then verify the vehicle, loan, income, VIN, and filing rules with current IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional.

    Phaseout example

    A single filer with $104,100 of MAGI and $2,400 of qualified interest has five $1,000 phaseout increments because the partial increment counts. The $1,000 reduction leaves an estimated $1,400 deduction before considering the rest of the tax return.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the maximum qualified car loan interest deduction?▼
    The annual maximum is $10,000 for tax years 2025 through 2028, before the modified adjusted gross income phaseout.
    Does interest on a used vehicle qualify?▼
    No. The vehicle's original use must begin with the taxpayer, so used-vehicle purchases do not qualify.
    At what income does the phaseout begin?▼
    It begins above $100,000 of MAGI for most filers and above $200,000 for married couples filing jointly. The reduction is $200 per $1,000 or portion of $1,000 above the threshold.
    Can I claim the deduction if I do not itemize?▼
    Eligible taxpayers may claim it whether or not they itemize, subject to all requirements and current IRS filing instructions.
    Does every dollar of interest on a qualifying vehicle loan count?▼
    Not necessarily. Related-party debt can be excluded, personal use must exceed 50%, and mixed-use, mixed-financing, or refinancing situations can require an allocation or limit the eligible loan balance.
