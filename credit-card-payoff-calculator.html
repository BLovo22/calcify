How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter Your Balance — your current credit card balance.
  2. Enter the APR — the annual percentage rate on your card. 20-25% is typical for most cards.
  3. Enter Monthly Payment — what you can pay each month. Even small increases dramatically shorten payoff time.
  4. Click Calculate — see months to payoff, total interest, and total cost.

Example: A $5,000 Balance at 20% APR

You have a $5,000 credit card balance at 20% APR.

💳 Payoff Comparison

  • At $150/month: 50 months (4.2 years), about $2,359 in interest
  • At $300/month: 20 months (1.7 years), about $907 in interest — saves about $1,452!
  • At $100/month: 109 months (9.1 years), about $5,840 in interest
  • Bottom line: paying more than the minimum saves thousands.

Common Mistakes

❌ Paying only a small fixed amount. A $5,000 balance at 20% APR with $100 monthly payments takes about 9 years and costs roughly $10,840 total.

❌ Adding new charges while paying off debt. If you keep swiping the card, you are running on a treadmill. Stop new charges until the balance is zero.

❌ Ignoring balance transfer offers. A 0% APR balance transfer card can save you thousands — but watch out for the 3-5% transfer fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long will it take to pay off my credit card?▼
It depends on your balance, APR, and monthly payment. A $5,000 balance at 20% APR with $150/month payments takes about 50 months and costs about $2,359 in interest. Increasing to $300/month cuts it to about 20 months and saves roughly $1,452.
What is the fastest way to pay off credit card debt?▼
The avalanche method (pay highest APR card first) saves the most money mathematically. The snowball method (pay smallest balance first) builds psychological momentum. Both work if you stick with it. Also consider a 0% APR balance transfer card or a debt consolidation loan at a lower rate.
