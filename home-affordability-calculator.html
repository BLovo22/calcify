How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your annual household income before taxes.
  2. Add monthly debt payments such as car loans, student loans, credit cards, and personal loans.
  3. Enter your down payment and mortgage assumptions.
  4. Include taxes, insurance, HOA, and PMI so your estimate reflects real housing costs.
  5. Click Calculate to see a practical home price estimate and monthly payment breakdown.

Example: $90,000 Income With $500 Monthly Debt

A buyer earning $90,000 per year has about $7,500 in gross monthly income. With $500 in existing debt, a $60,000 down payment, and a 6.5% mortgage rate, affordability depends heavily on total monthly housing costs.

🏡 Affordability Snapshot

  • Gross monthly income: $7,500
  • Target housing budget at 28%: $2,100/month
  • Total debt limit at 36%: $2,700/month
  • After $500 of existing debt, housing budget is capped near $2,200/month
  • Taxes, insurance, HOA, and PMI reduce the loan amount the same monthly budget can support.

Common Mistakes

Forgetting taxes and insurance. A mortgage payment without property taxes and insurance can make a home look more affordable than it really is.

Ignoring HOA fees. A $300 monthly HOA can reduce affordability by tens of thousands of dollars.

Underestimating PMI. If your down payment is below 20%, PMI can add a meaningful monthly cost.

Buying at the absolute maximum. Leave room for repairs, utilities, emergency savings, and retirement contributions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much house can I afford?▼
A common starting point is to keep housing costs near 28% of gross monthly income and total debt payments near 36%. Your actual number depends on income, debt, down payment, rate, taxes, insurance, HOA, PMI, and lender rules.
What payment does this calculator include?▼
It includes principal, interest, estimated property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA fees, and estimated PMI when your down payment is below 20%.
What is DTI?▼
Debt-to-income ratio compares monthly debt payments to gross monthly income. Lenders often use it to decide how much mortgage payment you may qualify for.
Does this replace lender pre-approval?▼
No. It is an educational estimate. A lender will also review your credit, assets, employment history, loan program, reserves, and local rules.
Should I use the maximum affordable price?▼
Usually, it is smarter to treat the result as an upper estimate and then choose a monthly payment that still leaves room for savings, repairs, and normal life.
