How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the Purchase and Loan Details — home price, down payment, mortgage rate, and actual loan term.
  2. Enter the Rental Alternative — current monthly rent and expected annual rent growth.
  3. Choose Years to Compare — this is independent of the loan term.
  4. Review Ownership Assumptions — taxes, insurance, maintenance, appreciation, and transaction costs materially change the result.
  5. Click Compare — compare rent paid with the buyer's net cost after estimated sale proceeds and remaining mortgage balance.

Example: 10 Years in a $400,000 Home vs. $2,000/Month Rent

You are deciding between buying a $400,000 home with 20% down at 6.5% on a 30-year loan or renting for $2,000/month.

🏠 10-Year Estimate

  • Monthly mortgage (P&I): ~$2,023
  • Estimated net buying cost: ~$215,000
  • Estimated rent with 3% annual increases: ~$275,000
  • Under the default assumptions, buying costs about $60,000 less.

Common Mistakes

❌ Comparing mortgage to rent 1:1. Property taxes, insurance, maintenance, transaction costs, and equity all matter.

❌ Assuming rents stay flat. At 3% annual growth, $2,000 rent becomes about $2,688 after 10 years.

❌ Treating the result as a forecast. Appreciation, repairs, rent growth, and selling costs are uncertain. Compare a conservative and an optimistic scenario.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to rent or buy?▼
There is no universal answer. The result depends on the purchase price, financing, ownership costs, expected time in the home, rent growth, appreciation, transaction costs, and what you could earn on money not used to buy.
How long until buying is cheaper?▼
There is no fixed break-even year. Change the comparison period and test conservative and optimistic assumptions to see when the estimated net buying cost becomes lower than rent in your scenario.
